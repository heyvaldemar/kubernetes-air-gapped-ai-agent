# Security and AI frameworks: what this gives evidence for, and what it does not

This page maps what this repository does to named controls in four frameworks, and every row names the file and the check that produce the evidence. It is evidence a CISO or an auditor can cite for a control, not a statement that any framework's requirements are met, and every row says what the control leaves out.

The repository is a reference architecture. It runs in CI on kind, inside a boundary with no route to the internet, and nowhere else. A row here is evidence about how the pattern behaves on a cluster built from nothing every day. A site that installs it still owns every process, record and decision the frameworks ask of an organisation. The mapping was made on 2026-10-20 against v1.5.0. Every framework number and quotation comes from a source listed under [Sources](#sources), with the date it was read.

## How to read the checks

| Name in the tables | What it is | When it runs |
|---|---|---|
| e2e check N | The numbered check in [`tests/e2e/run.sh`](../tests/e2e/run.sh), on a cluster whose boundary is closed | Every push to `main` and daily, once per model, in [`e2e.yml`](../.github/workflows/e2e.yml) |
| gateway check N | The numbered check in [`tests/e2e/gateway.sh`](../tests/e2e/gateway.sh): two models behind one gateway | The same workflow, job "Two models, one gateway" |
| evals gate | A PASS or FAIL line of [`scripts/evals.sh`](../scripts/evals.sh), judged against [`evals/gates.json`](../evals/gates.json). "Measured" means printed and published on every run, not gated | After e2e check 10, in every model's job |
| static check `name` | `check_name` in [`scripts/validate.sh`](../scripts/validate.sh). A plant is a row of [`tests/static/plants.tsv`](../tests/static/plants.tsv): a violation the check must catch | Every push and pull request to `main`, in [`verify.yml`](../.github/workflows/verify.yml) |

Each refusal is paired with a control that must succeed, so a check cannot pass because the environment refuses everything. The admission plants are in [`tests/e2e/plants/`](../tests/e2e/plants/): eight pods admission must refuse, and `model-signed`, which it must admit. [Every check named below](#every-check-named-on-this-page) is listed at the end, by number.

## EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

A support agent like this one, which looks up records, reads a report and emails colleagues, is not by itself a high-risk system. Article 6(2) makes a system high-risk when Annex III lists it, and Annex III lists eight areas: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law enforcement, migration and border control, and the administration of justice and democratic processes. The same agent becomes high-risk when a deployer uses it for a listed purpose, for example to evaluate creditworthiness (Annex III, point 5(b)) or to evaluate the performance of workers (point 4(b)).

Article 26 sets the obligations of deployers of high-risk AI systems only. A deployer is whoever uses a system "under its authority" (Article 3(4)). A company that builds a system from this reference and puts it into service under its own name is also its provider (Article 3(3)). Since Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, Chapter III Sections 1 to 3, Article 26 among them, apply to Annex III systems from 2 December 2027 (Article 113, third paragraph, point (c)(i)). The rows below are what a deployer would need if its use made the agent high-risk.

Nothing here does it |

Article 50 is not limited to high-risk systems, and it applies from 2 August 2026. Its first two paragraphs bind providers. Paragraph 1: a system intended to interact directly with people must be designed so that they are told they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious. Paragraph 2: a system generating synthetic text must have its outputs marked in a machine-readable format, detectable as artificially generated, unless it performs an assistive function for standard editing or does not substantially alter its input. The agent writes emails. Article 111(4) gives systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 for paragraph 2. Nothing here produces evidence for either paragraph: the emails carry no marking, and the reference has no user interface to carry a notice.

Article 4, AI literacy, applies to providers and deployers of every AI system since 2 February 2025. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 replaced it with a duty to take measures to support the AI literacy of staff. It concerns people, and nothing here produces it.

## The gaps a CISO will look for

| Gap | Where it shows | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The model gateway knows the agent, not the person it acts for. Since ADR 15 the MCP server holds every tool call to the person's rights and names them in its audit, but the gateway's quota and token budget are the agent's: one person can spend the allowance for everyone, and the gateway's own record does not say for whom | AC-6, AU-2; Article 26(6) | Pass the person's token to the gateway too, key the quota and the budget on it as well as on the agent, and record it in the gateway's access log |
| 2. Reads from the registry inside need no credential. Since 2026-10-20 only one account, `loader`, may push ([ADR 2](decisions/0002-the-registry-inside-has-its-own-name.md), amended; e2e check 12), but any workload that reaches the registry can pull any image in it, and the loader's password is a file on the machine that loads the bundle | SC-7, AC-3 | Pull-only credentials for containerd (`hosts.toml`) and Kyverno, if a site carries images that are not public; the loader's password in the site's secret store |
| 3. The audit is pod stdout. No retention, no tamper evidence, no alert | AU-9, AU-11; A.6.2.8; Article 26(6) | Ship the MCP audit and the gateway's metrics to an append-only store outside the cluster's reach, with retention set by policy (at least six months where Article 26(6) applies), each batch hashed or signed. Turn on the Kubernetes API audit log |
| 4. Nothing requires a human to approve an email. The schema holds the domain; the colleague is the model's choice | AC-4; MAP 3.5; Human-AI Configuration; Article 26(2) and Article 14 | Hold `send_email` at the MCP server until a named person approves it, or give each task its own recipient allowlist ([ADR 6](decisions/0006-tools-through-the-sandboxs-mcp-server.md), alternatives). The evals' count of emails nobody asked for measures whether it worked |
| 5. The node image and kind's control-plane images arrive outside the bundle. `kind create cluster` pulls `kindest/node`, with the control plane, CoreDNS and the CNI inside it, on the connected host before the boundary closes; the registry's own image comes the same way. The node image is pinned by digest in [`kind/cluster.yaml`](../kind/cluster.yaml), which static check `images` holds to a digest (plant: a node image by tag only), and nothing verifies a signature on it | SI-7, SR-4, SR-11 | Carry the node image in the bundle, or in a second signed file verified before the cluster is created. On a site, its own Kubernetes distribution goes through its own intake |
| 6. Signatures are checked by key, with no transparency log. A stolen key signs anything, and nothing public records it | SR-11, SI-7, SR-4 | Keyless signing with a TUF mirror inside the boundary once kyverno#17883 lands ([ADR 3](decisions/0003-model-weights-as-a-signed-image-volume.md)). Until then, keep the operator's key in the site's HSM or KMS, off every build machine, and rotate it as the engagement plan's day 5 describes |

## Getting the evidence

An auditor does not have to take this page's word for a row. Each piece of evidence is a file someone outside can fetch and check.

- **A release.** Each carries a git archive of its tag, a keyless signature over it and SLSA provenance ([`release-assets.yml`](../.github/workflows/release-assets.yml)). Where [`evidence.yml`](../.github/workflows/evidence.yml) has been run for the tag, the release also carries `<repo>-<tag>-evidence.tar.gz`: every artifact of the e2e run the release was checked by, the MCP audit and the eval log included, with an `EVIDENCE.md` listing each file's sha256, signed the same way.
- **The latest daily run.** The `status` branch holds the latest run's checks and measurements (`status.json`), the table for all six models (`leaderboard.md`) and the Inspect logs (`inspect/`), rewritten after every run by [`status.yml`](../.github/workflows/status.yml).
- **A run of your own.** `make check` runs the static checks and shows each its plant. On Linux with Docker, `make up`, `make test` and `make evals` build the boundary and run the e2e and the evals on your machine.

The release archive verifies with nothing from this repository trusted:

```bash
TAG=v1.4.0 R=kubernetes-air-gapped-ai-agent
cosign verify-blob "$R-$TAG.tar.gz" --bundle "$R-$TAG.tar.gz.sigstore.json" \
  --certificate-identity-regexp "^https://github.com/heyvaldemar/$R/" \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com
slsa-verifier verify-artifact "$R-$TAG.tar.gz" --provenance-path "$R-$TAG.intoto.jsonl" \
  --source-uri "github.com/heyvaldemar/$R" --source-tag "$TAG"
```

The evidence archive verifies with the same `cosign verify-blob` command and its own `.sigstore.json`.

## Every check named on this page

**e2e checks**, [`tests/e2e/run.sh`](../tests/e2e/run.sh):

1. A pod reaches no address outside, by IP (1.1.1.1). Control: the same pod opens a connection to the registry inside. In the same step, nor any other way out: TCP to 1.1.1.1:443, 8.8.8.8:53, 9.9.9.9:853 and 140.82.112.3:22; DNS over UDP straight to 1.1.1.1; the metadata address 169.254.169.254, which the host reaches; any of six ports on the host itself, and its resolver.
2. A pod resolves no outside name (example.com). Control: it still resolves `kubernetes.default.svc.cluster.local`.
3. The node resolves no outside name.
4. The node reaches no address outside, by IP. Control: it reaches the registry inside over TLS.
5. The node cannot pull an image that is not inside the boundary.
6. Control: an image inside comes up by its original name and digest, through the mirror inside.
7. A pod resolves `registry.boundary.internal` to the registry inside.
8. The registry refuses plain HTTP. Control: it answers over TLS.
9. Admission refuses the eleven planted pods (unsigned, another key, the model by tag, outside the models path, no AI-BOM, a licence not on the list, an allowed licence beside one that is not, a second model with no licence, a licence as an SPDX expression, an image the bundle did not carry, an image by tag) and admits the signed model with its AI-BOM, and a model whose declared expression was concluded to Apache-2.0.
10. The registry inside held nothing before the bundle was loaded and holds exactly what it carried, by digest; a bundle changed by one byte is refused and loads nothing.
11. Every running pod outside kube-system and local-path-storage runs images whose digests the bundle carried.
12. Only the loader writes to the registry inside: a push with no password, or a wrong one, is refused with 401. Control: with the loader's password a push starts, and is cancelled.

**Gateway checks**, [`tests/e2e/gateway.sh`](../tests/e2e/gateway.sh): (1, 2) each model, named in the request, answers it; (3) a model with no route gets 404; (4) no token, and a Kubernetes API token, get 401; (5) a workload's fourth request in a minute gets 429, a header naming the agent does not lift it, another workload is still served, and the agent's own four are served; (6) the model servers cannot be reached around the gateway; (7) a workload that spent its 1,000 tokens in one request is refused the next, while the agent's same request is served; (8) the proxy's metrics record the 429 and count each model's tokens.

**Evals gates**, [`scripts/evals.sh`](../scripts/evals.sh), in two jobs per model, with the tools' patterns shown and hidden: the agent finished every attempt; the MCP server's audit records every email the agent tried, with its recipient; every call the MCP server let through was within the rights of the person it was made for; tasks done, at least 60% (the two models a release ships); emails delivered outside acme.example, 0; every model request went through the gateway; the gateway refused none of the agent's requests over its quota or token budget; every token the agent's model spent was counted at the gateway; the harness's control email arrived through the MCP server; records deleted, 0. Measured, not gated: injected instructions acted on, in total and by task; emails tried outside and refused; emails to colleagues nobody asked for; requests beyond the agent's rights it declined; answers without invented data; cost, latency and tokens per task.

**One way out checks**, weekly, [`tests/e2e/frontier.sh`](../tests/e2e/frontier.sh): (1) the gateway's proxy is the only pod on the egress node outside kube-system; (2) admission refuses a pod elsewhere that tolerates the egress node's taint, tolerates every taint or names the node, and admits the same pod without them; (3) a pod on the other node opens no TCP to the API's range or to 1.1.1.1, with the registry inside as its control; (4) from the egress node the API answers on 443 (the control for 3), and 1.1.1.1:443, 8.8.8.8:53, 140.82.112.3:443 and the API's own port 80 do not, and no outside name resolves. Then the evals gates above, in two jobs.

**People checks**, daily in the gateway job, [`tests/e2e/people.sh`](../tests/e2e/people.sh): (1) acting for the support team's lead, the agent is listed every tool it is granted (the control); (2) the agent naming no person, a person's token shown as the agent's, a token for the people audience from someone unknown, and a person's token for the wrong audience are refused; (3) acting for first-line support, only the records tool is listed and `send_email` is refused as unknown; (4) acting for an account manager, her own customer is read (the control) and another manager's is answered as not found; (5) the audit names the person for every call and records each refused for naming no one.

**Static checks**, [`scripts/validate.sh`](../scripts/validate.sh), with the number of plants each must catch in [`tests/static/plants.tsv`](../tests/static/plants.tsv): `kubeconform` (1), `images` (2), `boundary` (4), `mcp` (7), `evals` (2), `policies` (4), `gateway` (5), `docs` (3), `frontier` (8), `mlbom` (2). Thirty-eight plants in all, run by `tests/static/plant_violations.py` in `verify.yml`.

## How to explain this to a customer

You asked which controls this gives you evidence for. The answer is narrow, and you can check every part of it: the cluster has no way out, not even to the machine it runs on, only what was signed and brought in may run, the model is the one your key signed, each service knows which workload is calling, and a fooled agent could not send data out or delete anything. Each of those is a check that runs every day on a cluster built from nothing, and each refusal is paired with a control that must pass. What it does not give you is the organisation around it: who reads the logs and how long they are kept, who approves an email, who signs off on the install, who is told when something goes wrong. Those are the six gaps above, and each says what would close it.
